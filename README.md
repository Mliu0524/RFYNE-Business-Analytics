Project title:
RFYNE Business Analytics 

Business problem:
RFYNE case study is inspired by my small business and my love and appreciation for vintage designer pieces.
As the owner of RFYNE continue to invest in her business. She wants to know the questions below: 
1. How is the business doing overall? Growing or declining?
2. Should we continue to source from multiple countries? What are the best sourcing countries?
3. What are the top performing brands? Any brands we should stop sourcing?
4. What's our inventory looking like? The items/ brands that holds a lot of stale inventory?
5. What brands should we buy more in the future? what should we consider slow down on buying?
6. For certain brands that don't perform well, what's the customer journey looks like? What can we improve?
7. Which Campaign generates the most profit in a certain timeframe? The most effective campaign?
8. What's the selling platform/ source brings in most sales? Most Profitable?




Tools used:
Excel, Tableau, SQL


Key insights:
I evaluated brand sourcing decisions using a rolling 12-month performance window while monitoring recent 3-month trends.

1. We saw a huge 510% month over month growth in revenue this Month (July) Since store was open, we see steady growth in both revenue and profit based
   on running total of revenue and profit. RFYNE is doing well with a huge spike in sales volume this month.
   
2. Revenue and Profit for France and Italy have dropped significantly comparing to a year ago. However, profit and ROI remain similar. Meaning sourcing from
   Italy and France don't affect the profitability and efficiency of the business.

3. This month's top performing brands by profit in each category are Fendi, Vivienne Westwood, Miss sixty and Longchamp. Top performing brands by AOV are
   Fendi, Jean Paul Gaultier, Furla and Diesel. Top performers by ROI are Vivienne Westwood, Longchamp, Prada and miss sixty.

4. There are currently 489 items left in the inventory. It's bit concerning as stale inventory takes up to 92% of the entire inventory.
   Louis Vuitton items seem to move the slowest based off having the lowest sell through rate (59.52%) and the most amount of stale inventory.
   I've created a breakdown of what the stale items are, so we can work on discounting them, as well as researching why they went stale.

5. By examining profit margin vs sales matrix, we want to prioritize high return and high margin brands like Fendi, Prada, Gucci. High margin and low return (hidden gems) work on improving sales, brands like Jean Paul Gaultier, Vivienne Westwood, Just Cavali and Ferragamo. For the low margin and low return brands like Chanel, Dior and Coach, consider slow down on investing as we witnessed negative margin for the past 3 months. For the mid margin and mid return, we shall continue to monitor this matrix as well as increasing investment.

6. Brands like Louis Vuitton, Dior and Fornarina that don't perform as well, I did analysis on customer journey. More customers tend to leave in the phase between sending offers and making purchase. Experiment implementing email reminder or follow-up after offer was sent can be effective on improving offer to purchase rate. However, offer to purchase rate is the lowest among across all brands.
   
7. The most profitable and effective campaign is luxury_bag_edit for having the highest profit and AOV for serval month (Jan-June 2026) followed by summer_y2k_sale. Need to work on improving archive_collection campaign, as its profit, AOV and ROAS is on the lower side along with higher CPM. Consider improving campaign or reallocating money for better performing campaign.

8. In the most recent month, Instagram generates the most amount of sales, Depop has the highest profit margin. I spot the same situation from Jan to July 2026. RFYNE seem to get healthy margin selling cheaper products in high volume. Ebay order generally has higher AOV, pricer item but need to work on improving margins.
Instagram generates the most revenue.
